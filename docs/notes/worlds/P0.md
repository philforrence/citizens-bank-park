# P0: one people system for the park, and the crowd's cost

Wave 2's P0. Worktree branch `worktree-agent-a759c9acce9bab400`, on top of `ballpark` at `02db415`.

Wave 1 left three figure systems for people seen close up (W1's `gate3b/Folk*`, W3's `alley/Folk.js`, W2's `places/Cast.js`)
and the plaza's and the concourse's people over their budget. Now there's one: **Cast.js**, a single pool that every
place's people are drawn from, and the gate's and the Alley's people are on it with all their acts, props and stories.

## What moved, and what was removed

**Cast.js stays the system** (it was the most complete: painted faces, 2008 clothing with the right name and number backs,
props, three figures). Underneath, it's now **one pool for the park** (details in "How it draws"). Every `new Cast( { parent, max } )`
is a troupe of slots in it; the API is unchanged.

**The Third Base Gate** (`gate3b/Folk.js`): its own figure (2,100 triangles), rig (`FolkRig.js`, JS and WGSL), atlas and
shaders are gone. `Folk.js` is now a thin hand-over: the plaza's code (`Arrivals`, `Cast`, `Vendors`, the police, `TV`,
`Tailgate`, `Celebration`, `Store`, the statue) still moves and poses the same figure objects, with the same angles, 24-bit
looks and prop bits, and each is drawn as a Cast person:
- the look becomes Cast's (the same clothes: the takers' navy Phillies jackets, the bag check's red STAFF jackets, the
  troopers' grey and campaign hats, the police's leather and shields or yellow raincoats, FOX 29's crew, McFadden's
  SECURITY bouncer, the store's red polos; and now a face, a build, the cold in the cheeks, a scarf, gloves);
- the angles become Cast's pose (the same axes: the stride, the arms' swing against it, sitting, astride a horse);
- the prop bits become what's in each hand and what's worn.

**Ashburn Alley** (`alley/Folk.js`): its 19 pairs of pose meshes (38 draws), its props draw, atlases and shaders are gone.
`Folk.js` is a thin hand-over with the same `add( pair, ... )`, `hold()`, `k`, `handAt()`: each of the pairs' poses
(joint positions) is solved once, at build, into Cast's angles (the arms to the same hands and elbows, the trunk's lean,
the head, the legs, the drop onto a bench or into a crouch) and a person is drawn between the two by `k`; walkers use
Cast's own stride. The homemade signs and the Bull's 8 x 10 go into the park's sign atlas.

**Kept as they were:** every character, act, prop, interaction and clock: the arrivals and the queues at the eight

## The API (Cast.js's header documents it)

Unchanged, and added to (every old call works):
```js
import { Cast, TOP, COLOR, HAT, CHEST, BACK, GEAR, PROP, restPose, seat, sign, lookAt } from './Cast.js';
const cast = new Cast( { parent: this.group, max: 200 } );   // a troupe (parent: the place's group, the field frame)
const p = cast.add( look );          // packLook's fields: skin, hair, hairStyle, facial, glasses, female, age, build,
                                     // top, color, sleeves, back, chest, pants, shoes, hat, poncho, scarf, gloves,
                                     // badge, gear, dry, seed (B's lanyard at z bit 18 is kept for it)
p.x, p.y, p.z, p.yaw, p.scale, p.visible, p.pose, p.fresh
p.lod                                // (read) how they were drawn last frame: 0 near, 1 far, 2 distant, -1 not at all
cast.setLook( p, look );  cast.update();  cast.list;  cast.bounds = new Sphere( ... );
seat( pose, h );  sign( ( ctx, w, h ) => ..., name );  lookAt( key, { x, y, z, r, k } | null );
```
- **The pose** gains `varL` / `varR` (a prop's variant: a sign's card, an umbrella's colours, a camera's colour).
- **New props:** `umbrella`, `furled`, `sign`, `scanner`, `flashlight`, `mic`, `tickets`, `cowbell`, `tongs`, `pennant`,
  `photo`, `cigarette`, `radio`, `thermos` (C asked for it). `peanuts`, `bag` in the right hand too; `towel`, `program`,
  `sandwich`, `phone` in the left too.
- **New tops:** `camo`, `flyers`, `trooper`, `coat` (long), `polo` (a turtleneck under it when the sleeves differ),
  `vendor` (canvas money apron), `raincoat` (long, reflective). **Colours:** `camel`, `policeYellow`, `trooperGrey`, `midnight`.
- **New hats:** `capWhite`, `police` (peaked, the shield), `campaign` (a trooper's, its own geometry), `cabbie`, `capWS`.
- **Worn gear** (`look.gear`): `GEAR.messenger`, `backpack`, `radio`, `credential`. A police **badge** (`look.badge`).
  **`look.dry`**: under cover (the rain's not on them).
- **Backs:** `LUZINSKI`, `MCNABB`, `UPTON`, `JENKINS`, `COSTE`, and words across a back: `STAFF`, `SECURITY`, `POLICE`, `FOX29`.

## How it draws

- **One pool.** Every troupe's people are slots in one pool: **one pipeline** for the figures (three meshes sharing a
  material: the near, far and distant figures, each told its stretch of the order by its draw's params) and one for the
  contact shadows, **4 draws for every person in the park**. Wave 1's people had 10 pipelines and up to ~45 draws (the
  Alley's 38 alone).
- **Once a frame, as it's drawn** (the first of its meshes' `onBeforeRender`, with the camera of that pass): everyone
  outside the view is culled (a sphere against the frustum's sides; a troupe's `bounds` first); the figure is picked by
  **how tall they stand on the screen** (near above 14% of its height, distant below 4%), so a TV camera's 5° lens sees the
  backdrop behind home at the far figure, not the 3-sided distant one, and a person 12 m off in a 60° view doesn't pay for
  a nose and thumbs; the near ones are sorted front to back; only those seen are packed and uploaded.
- **Drawn first** (render order -1) and the near ones front to back: on an immediate-mode GPU the floor and walls behind
  them are rejected early (on this M2, a tile GPU, hidden surfaces are removed whatever the order).
- **Two pose buffers turn about**: last frame's is the previous pose for the motion vectors, not uploaded again.
- **The shader:** the parts a person hasn't got and the props not in hand fold to a point before any rigging (so forty
  props cost next to nothing on the people not holding them); the far and distant figures rig once (only their walk goes
  into the motion vectors); the palette comes from a buffer, not an array in every invocation; looks are `vec4u`.
- **CPU:** `p.lod` lets the places move the unseen and the distant less often (the concourse: the unseen every 4th frame
  and the distant every 3rd, by that much time; the gate's and the Alley's hand-overs pose the unseen every 4th frame).

## The numbers

**How they were measured.** Under the lock's exclusive mode, the full park at 1280 x 800, five views: the plaza (the start
view at first pitch, the 27th), the concourse behind home (looking toward third, the 5th inning, the 27th), the concourse
along 125-128, the Alley (the 29th) and the TV center camera. In each view the people were shown and hidden in turn, four
times, 40 frames each after 12 to settle (`__qa.profile()`), and the medians compared, so the GPU's drift over the run
cancels. "Everyone" is every close-up person in view; "its own" is only that place's troupe (the gate's on the plaza).
The *before* is the base commit (`02db415`) run from an export of its tree, the same job the same way; the *after* is this
branch at `d22ce35`. The job generator is in the worktree (`.claude/p0/mkprof3.mjs`), the results in
`/Users/phillipforrence/citizens-bank-park/.claude/qa/p0/prof/` (`base.result.txt`, `final.result.txt`).

**GPU, ms per frame the people cost** (median of four on/off pairs):

| View | Before: everyone (its own) | After: everyone (its own) |
|---|---|---|
| The plaza | 0.73 (0.49, the gate's) | 0.97 (0.29, the gate's) |
| The concourse behind home | **3.39** (3.22) | **0.64** (0.53) |
| The concourse along 125-128 | **2.55** (2.55) | **0.61** (0.57) |
| The Alley | 0.72 (0.18) | 0.58 (0.58) |
| TV center | 0.47 | 0.00 |

- **The concourse is back within budget, from 3.4 ms to 0.6.** The plaza's people are within it too.
- The before run was noisy (the frame times drifted 5 ms over it, which is why I switched to paired medians; the plaza's
  and the Alley's before-diffs range from -1.4 to +1.2 ms, so read those two rows as "under ~1 ms" either way). The after
  run was steady (pairs within ±0.15 ms).
- **The plaza, everyone 0.97:** the gate's own people are 0.29 of it; the rest is ~600 of the other places' people seen
  through the gate as the distant figure (the concourse's and the Alley's, mostly behind the stands). This M2 is a tile GPU:
  hidden surfaces cost no shading whatever the draw order, but their vertices still run.
- **The Alley** now draws its people at Cast's detail (faces, props in hand): 0.58 ms for 225 of them in view, where its
  own low-poly figures cost 0.18 (noisy). It's within the ~1 ms a place gets.
- **TV center:** the pool culls the concourse's people the center-field lens can't see; the old systems drew them all.
- **Where the concourse's saving came from:** the figure picked by screen size (94 near figures before, 40 after, in the
  view behind home; a person 12 m off no longer pays for a nose and thumbs), culling to the view (the troupe's 350 drawn
  whatever the camera faced before), the far figures rigged once, the hidden props and parts folded before the rig, the
  palette out of the invocations' arrays, and the rig reading its rows without copying the pose. The near figures alone
  measure within noise of zero now (-0.23 and +0.13 ms with them left out).

**Draws, triangles, pipelines** (`meshRenderer.stats`, the plaza view, full park): draws 771 → 755, triangles 6.49 M → 6.41 M,
**pipelines 598 → 590**. The people's own pipelines went from 10 (the concourse's cast 4, the gate's Folk 3, the Alley's
folk, props and contact shadows 3) to **2** for the whole park, however many places add people; their draws from up to
~45 to 4.

**Loading:** 9.6 s → 7.8 s warm in the profile jobs (the desk's pages; the difference is mostly noise). The cold load
(~30 s, 598 pipelines to compile in a fresh browser) couldn't be measured here (the desk's browser keeps its cache); it
has 8 fewer pipelines to compile, and the people's two are compiled behind the loading screen like the rest. The Alley's
poses are solved at build (~40 ms); the gate's and the Alley's builds are otherwise as they were.

**CPU, ms per frame in the places' updates** (measured in the page during the same profile jobs, while the people were
shown; each place's `update()` timed):

| View | Before: all places (the concourse, the gate, the Alley) | After |
|---|---|---|
| The plaza | 1.61 (0.82, 0.42, 0.29) | 0.85 (0.44, 0.25, 0.12) |
| The concourse behind home | 1.88 (1.37, 0.19, 0.27) | 1.04 (0.78, 0.13, 0.09) |
| The concourse along | 1.96 (1.45, 0.20, 0.26) | 0.77 (0.55, 0.10, 0.09) |

The after also pays the pool's once-a-frame pack and upload (in the render, not in a place): 0.08-0.19 ms for ~1,100
people (Node, `flush`). The CPU was shared with other sessions during both runs, so read these as roughly halved, not to
the hundredth.

## For the merger

- **Cast.js is rewritten underneath** (the API is the same). B and C add to it in marked blocks, so expect conflicts there:
  - **B's ticket lanyard:** B packs `o.lanyard` at z bit 18 in `packLook`; mine already does (take my line), and B's
    surface block goes in as is after `c = mix( c, printC, printA );`. The look is a `vec4u` now (it was floats), which
    B's `( lk.z >> 18u ) & 1u` reads the same.
  - B and C were given id ranges for anything they add (above); if either added a prop, top or hat in the old file,
    renumber it into its range.
  - B sets `cast.near` / `cast.far` each frame: harmless now (settable, unused).
- **C subclasses `ConcoursePeople`** and imports W2's and W1's modules (listed in ASK-C.md): all kept. I changed only
  `ConcoursePeople.update()`'s far-update rule (it reads `p.lod`), and `Concourse3B` now sets `cast.bounds` rather than
  `cast.mesh.boundingSphere` (C should do the same for its troupe).
- **A** calls `lookAt( 'phanatic', ... )` (it's a no-op on A's branch until this one is in).
- **The rain's cover** is the app's now; `Concourse3B` no longer builds it (a place that relied on it being there only
  with the concourse in scope gets it always).
- **Crowd.js, tools/gpu/ and the other builders' places:** untouched.
