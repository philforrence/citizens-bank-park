# P0: one people system for the park, and the crowd's cost

Wave 2's P0. Worktree branch `worktree-agent-a759c9acce9bab400`, on top of `ballpark` at `02db415`.

Wave 1 left three figure systems for people seen close up (W1's `gate3b/Folk*`, W3's `alley/Folk.js`, W2's `places/Cast.js`)
and the plaza's and the concourse's people over their budget. Now there's one: **Cast.js**, a single pool that every
place's people are drawn from, and the gate's and the Alley's people are on it with all their acts, props and stories.

## What moved, and what was removed

**Cast.js stays the system** (it was the most complete: painted faces, 2008 clothing with the right name and number backs,
props, three figures). Underneath, it's now **one pool for the park** (details in "How it draws"). Every `new Cast( { parent, max } )`
is a troupe of slots in it; the API is unchanged.

**The Third Base Gate** (`gate3b/Folk.js`): its own figure (2,100 triangles), rig (`FolkRig.js`, JS and WGSL), atlas and
shaders are gone. `Folk.js` is now a thin hand-over: the plaza's code (`Arrivals`, `Cast`, `Vendors`, the police, `TV`,
`Tailgate`, `Celebration`, `Store`, the statue) still moves and poses the same figure objects, with the same angles, 24-bit
looks and prop bits, and each is drawn as a Cast person:
- the look becomes Cast's (the same clothes: the takers' navy Phillies jackets, the bag check's red STAFF jackets, the
  troopers' grey and campaign hats, the police's leather and shields or yellow raincoats, FOX 29's crew, McFadden's
  SECURITY bouncer, the store's red polos; and now a face, a build, the cold in the cheeks, a scarf, gloves);
- the angles become Cast's pose (the same axes: the stride, the arms' swing against it, sitting, astride a horse);
- the prop bits become what's in each hand and what's worn.

**Ashburn Alley** (`alley/Folk.js`): its 19 pairs of pose meshes (38 draws), its props draw, atlases and shaders are gone.
`Folk.js` is a thin hand-over with the same `add( pair, ... )`, `hold()`, `k`, `handAt()`: each of the pairs' poses
(joint positions) is solved once, at build, into Cast's angles (the arms to the same hands and elbows, the trunk's lean,
the head, the legs, the drop onto a bench or into a crouch) and a person is drawn between the two by `k`; walkers use
Cast's own stride. The homemade signs and the Bull's 8 x 10 go into the park's sign atlas.

**Kept as they were:** every character, act, prop, interaction and clock: the arrivals and the queues at the eight
lanes, the ticket scans and the bag check, the towels, the scalpers, the peanut sales, the shirt man, the police and
the horses (the kid patting the black one's nose), FOX 29 live with the fans jumping into the shot, the tailgaters,

## The API (Cast.js's header documents it)

Unchanged, and added to (every old call works):
```js
import { Cast, TOP, COLOR, HAT, CHEST, BACK, GEAR, PROP, restPose, seat, sign, lookAt } from './Cast.js';
const cast = new Cast( { parent: this.group, max: 200 } );   // a troupe (parent: the place's group, the field frame)
const p = cast.add( look );          // packLook's fields: skin, hair, hairStyle, facial, glasses, female, age, build,
                                     // top, color, sleeves, back, chest, pants, shoes, hat, poncho, scarf, gloves,
                                     // badge, gear, dry, seed (B's lanyard at z bit 18 is kept for it)
p.x, p.y, p.z, p.yaw, p.scale, p.visible, p.pose, p.fresh
p.lod                                // (read) how they were drawn last frame: 0 near, 1 far, 2 distant, -1 not at all
cast.setLook( p, look );  cast.update();  cast.list;  cast.bounds = new Sphere( ... );
seat( pose, h );  sign( ( ctx, w, h ) => ..., name );  lookAt( key, { x, y, z, r, k } | null );
```
- **The pose** gains `varL` / `varR` (a prop's variant: a sign's card, an umbrella's colours, a camera's colour).
- **New props:** `umbrella`, `furled`, `sign`, `scanner`, `flashlight`, `mic`, `tickets`, `cowbell`, `tongs`, `pennant`,
  `photo`, `cigarette`, `radio`, `thermos` (C asked for it). `peanuts`, `bag` in the right hand too; `towel`, `program`,
  `sandwich`, `phone` in the left too.
- **New tops:** `camo`, `flyers`, `trooper`, `coat` (long), `polo` (a turtleneck under it when the sleeves differ),
  `vendor` (canvas money apron), `raincoat` (long, reflective). **Colours:** `camel`, `policeYellow`, `trooperGrey`, `midnight`.
- **New hats:** `capWhite`, `police` (peaked, the shield), `campaign` (a trooper's, its own geometry), `cabbie`, `capWS`.
- **Worn gear** (`look.gear`): `GEAR.messenger`, `backpack`, `radio`, `credential`. A police **badge** (`look.badge`).
  **`look.dry`**: under cover (the rain's not on them).
- **Backs:** `LUZINSKI`, `MCNABB`, `UPTON`, `JENKINS`, `COSTE`, and words across a back: `STAFF`, `SECURITY`, `POLICE`, `FOX29`.

## How it draws

- **One pool.** Every troupe's people are slots in one pool: **one pipeline** for the figures (three meshes sharing a
  material: the near, far and distant figures, each told its stretch of the order by its draw's params) and one for the
  contact shadows, **4 draws for every person in the park**. Wave 1's people had 11 pipelines and up to ~45 draws (the
  Alley's 38 alone).
- **Once a frame, as it's drawn** (the first of its meshes' `onBeforeRender`, with the camera of that pass): everyone
  outside the view is culled (a sphere against the frustum's sides; a troupe's `bounds` first); the figure is picked by
  **how tall they stand on the screen** (near above 14% of its height, distant below 4%), so a TV camera's 5° lens sees the
  backdrop behind home at the far figure, not the 3-sided distant one, and a person 12 m off in a 60° view doesn't pay for
  a nose and thumbs; the near ones are sorted front to back; only those seen are packed and uploaded.
- **Drawn first** (render order -1): people stand in front of most of what's behind them, so the floor and walls behind
  them are rejected early.
- **Two pose buffers turn about**: last frame's is the previous pose for the motion vectors, not uploaded again.
- **The shader:** the parts a person hasn't got and the props not in hand fold to a point before any rigging (so forty
  props cost next to nothing on the people not holding them); the far and distant figures rig once (only their walk goes
  into the motion vectors); the palette comes from a buffer, not an array in every invocation; looks are `vec4u`.
- **CPU:** `p.lod` lets the places move the unseen and the distant less often (the concourse: the unseen every 4th frame
  and the distant every 3rd, by that much time; the gate's and the Alley's hand-overs pose the unseen every 4th frame).
