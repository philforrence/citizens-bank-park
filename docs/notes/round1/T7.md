# T7: the people on the field (players, umpires, gear, routines)

Branch: `worktree-agent-a70d5e197d867e819` (3 commits on top of `62f9449`).
Files: `src/ballpark/game/Looks.js` (new), `Players.js`, `Rig.js`, `Motions.js`, `Director.js`. **I touched no shared files**
(BallparkApp.js, geo.js and the rest are untouched). `Rig.js` exports new `PART` codes and `OPTIONAL`, and `bakePose`
now leaves out all the optional pieces. The statues in Exterior.js and Landmarks.js call it, and their `drop` codes are unchanged.

I was asked to wrap up early, so the list below has 8 changes, not 15. What's left is in "Unfinished" (the motions for
several of them are already written).

## Changes

1. **Every player his own face** (Looks.js plus the shader). Every man in the box score has his real skin tone,
   hair colour and beard: Werth's scruffy short beard (it also has a little thickness), Lidge's goatee, Hamels
   clean-shaven, Rollins' goatee and moustache, Hinske's red beard, Howard's chin strap, Ruiz's chin beard. Victorino,
   Werth, Crawford, Upton, Longoria and Perez wear eye black. Older men (the umpires, the managers) go grey at the
   temples and have lines round the eyes. Jaws are broader or narrower. Faces are shaded with a shadow under the nose
   and the lower lip, a darker underside to the jaw and some colour in the cheeks. Before this, skin was 4 random tones
   by hash, so Howard and Rollins could come out white. The rosters are real. Who wears eye black and the beard styles
   of the lesser-known players are plausible inventions.
2. **Every player his own build** (Rig.solvePose). Each player's height scales his bones against the mesh's 6'2" base,
   and his girth thickens the trunk and limbs (shoulders and hips widen too). A belly is pushed out in the vertex hook.
   Examples: Rollins 5'8", Victorino and Iwamura 5'9", Madson and Price 6'6", Howard 6'4" and broad, Blanton and Stairs
   with bellies, the umpires thick in the middle. The head grows only a third as fast as the body. The pitcher's release
   point and the catcher's glove target are divided by the player's scale, so the ball still meets the hand.
3. **Uniforms worn each player's way.**
   - Legs: high red or navy socks with stirrups (Rollins, Victorino, Ruiz, Bruntlett, Crawford, Bartlett, Navarro)
     or long pants, looser at the hem, down over the shoe tops.
   - Arms: long, elbow-length or short undershirt sleeves; wristbands.
   - Hands: batting gloves in each player's own two team colours (palm, strap and tab). The batter wears an elbow
     guard on his front arm (Utley, Werth, Crawford, Peña). Some fielders have batting gloves hanging out of a back pocket.
   - Team jackets (Phillies red, Rays navy, zip and knit collar and cuffs, white script on the chest) are supported via
     `ROLE.jacket` but not yet worn by anyone.
   - The MLB batterman logo is on the back of every cap and helmet.
   - The Rays' chest wordmark now has its gold burst.
   - A Phanatic back ("PHANATIC" and a star) is drawn for when he appears.
4. **Helmets and gear.**
   - Batting helmets get their ear flaps on the right side: left for right-handed hitters, right for lefties, both for
     switch hitters (Rollins, Victorino, Navarro and others). Before, a lefty's flap was on the wrong ear.
   - Helmets are glossy with scuffs; some have pine tar on the crown.
   - The catcher wears a backwards skull cap.
   - The mask used by the catcher and the plate umpire has a curved steel frame, bars, a throat guard and leather pads.
     It replaces the floating grid box.
   - The chest protector is a shaped plate curving round the chest, with shoulder caps and a belly flap. Shin guards
     are shells with a knee cap and instep flap, plus knee savers.
   - Gloves by position: the catcher's round mitt with laced rim, the first baseman's long scoop, fielders' gloves.
   - The plate umpire has ball bags on his hips.
   - Bats vary (natural, black, two-tone, cherry) with pine tar on the handle.
5. **Dirt that builds, rain that soaks.**
   - Clay is ground into the cloth where each man goes down: the catcher all over, infielders' knees and fronts (with a
     grass smear), the pitcher's drag knee, the seat and legs of anyone who slid (steals and extra-base advances come
     from the play data).
   - Every pant leg gets some dust.
   - Dirt builds faster in the first night's mud, and the uniforms are clean again on the 29th.
   - In the downpour, cloth soaks darker, the white jerseys go pinkish over the red undershirts, skin shines, rain beads
     on helmets, and cleats are caked with mud.
   - The players' material defines `DRY`, so the engine's generic wet gloss no longer makes the cloth shiny.
6. **The pitcher's routine and the catcher's signs.** The pitcher leans in from the rubber for the sign, with his glove
   on his knee and the ball hidden by his back thigh. About 1 pitch in 5 he shakes one off. He nods, then comes set. The
   catcher puts the fingers down between his thighs with the glove over his knee, then gives the target.
7. **The batter's routine.** After every pitch that doesn't end the at-bat, the batter steps out of the box. He tugs his
   batting gloves' straps with the bat hanging from his hand, taps the plate as he steps back in, then goes into his stance.
8. **The umpires' calls.** The plate umpire rises for a called strike (strike three is punched out; a swinging third
   strike gets hammered) and puts both arms up on a foul. Base umpires hammer the outs at their bags. The line umpire
   twirls a finger on a home run. Umpire faces and builds are individual (older, heavier; one moustache).

## Noticed in other areas (for review)

- **Wet-cloth gloss:** the generic `frame.wet` in lighting.js makes every up-facing non-DRY surface glossy (roughness
  0.14). That suits concrete but not fabric, so other cloth-like things (crowd ponchos aside, flags, tarp cover) may look
  plasticky in the rain.
- **Home jersey reads grey in the TV shot:** from center field by day, the Phillies' white jerseys look grey-blue (long
  lens plus haze).
